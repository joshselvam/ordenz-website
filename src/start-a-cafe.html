This guide is general information, not legal or tax advice. Rules change and differ by state, city and council. Check with the official sites below, your local authority and an accountant before you apply or decide.

Opening a cafe in India

  1. Choose how you'll run it. Most small cafes start as a sole proprietorship or partnership; an LLP or private limited company separates your personal and business money. Open a current account in the business name.
  2. FSSAI food registration or licence. Every food business needs one before it opens. From April 2026, Basic Registration covers a turnover of up to ₹1.5 crore a year and a State Licence covers ₹1.5 crore to ₹50 crore. Apply on the FSSAI FoSCoS portal. Your FSSAI number prints on every OrdenZ bill.
  3. Shop and establishment registration under your state's law, usually within a few weeks of opening. It's done online in most states.
  4. Trade licence from your municipal corporation or panchayat. Depending on your city and size, you may also need a fire safety certificate, a signboard permission or an eating-house licence. Ask the local office what applies to your address.
  5. GST, when you need it. A restaurant usually registers once its turnover passes ₹20 lakh a year (₹10 lakh in some states). Most standalone cafes then charge 5% GST without input tax credit. Small cafes up to ₹1.5 crore can choose the composition scheme instead: a flat 5% of turnover, which you can't add to the customer's bill.
  6. Payments. Ask your bank for a free UPI merchant QR to start. For automatic confirmation of each UPI payment on the kiosk, apply for a Razorpay or PhonePe merchant account; approval takes a few days.

In OrdenZ: choose India when you sign up. Add your GSTIN and FSSAI number under House, and set the GST rate (5% for most cafes). Bills show CGST and SGST by themselves once the GSTIN is saved; until then they are plain bills with no GST.

Opening a cafe in Malaysia

  1. Register the business with SSM: a sole proprietorship or partnership through ezBiz, or a Sdn Bhd through MyCoID.
  2. Premises and signboard licence from your local council (PBT), for example DBKL in Kuala Lumpur. Councils check the premises, so apply before you renovate too far.
  3. Food handler training and typhoid vaccination for everyone who handles food, from a course accredited by the Ministry of Health (KKM).
  4. Fire certificate (BOMBA) if your premises is classed as a designated premises. Check with your state fire department.
  5. Halal certification from JAKIM if you want to market the cafe as halal. It's optional, but many customers look for it.
  6. Service tax, when you need it. Food and beverage businesses register for service tax once their sales pass RM1.5 million a year, and charge 6%.
  7. E-invoices. From 1 September 2026, businesses with annual sales under RM3 million don't have to issue e-invoices, but may do so voluntarily.
  8. Payments. Ask your bank for a DuitNow QR to start at the counter. For automatic confirmation of each DuitNow payment on the kiosk, apply for a Fiuu Offline Payment merchant account; approval is with the provider.

In OrdenZ: choose Malaysia when you sign up. Prices are in RM and cash is rounded to 5 sen. Add your SSM number, and your SST number once you register; SST is only charged after that. Below the threshold leave SST blank. Set a service charge if you have one. Connect Fiuu under House for automatic DuitNow QR, or record a received DuitNow at the counter. Reports → e-Invoice prepares MyInvois files for your provider when you need them.

Just the counter

Free is enough for one counter in a browser. That's enough to bill, send orders to the kitchen and see your sales. A kitchen phone with a staff PIN is not a paired device.

Receipt and kitchen printer

A Bluetooth or network thermal printer, 58 mm or 80 mm. Buy one cash drawer that opens from the printer. Test your exact model with OrdenZ before you buy several.

The basics

Reliable internet

A broadband line plus a mobile hotspot as backup. Offline counter billing is on Starter and Pro. Free needs a connection. UPI, DuitNow and table QR always need internet.

Pricing your menu

  • Know the cost of every plate. Add a recipe to each item in OrdenZ (Board → Edit recipe) and the menu shows its ingredient cost and food-cost percentage, updated with every supplier bill.
  • Use food cost as a guide. A common rule of thumb for cafes is to keep ingredient cost around 25–35% of the selling price. Coffee and tea are usually lower, cooked food higher.
  • Remember the extras: cups, lids and packaging, delivery-app commission (OrdenZ lets you set separate app prices), and payment charges.
  • Start small. 20 to 30 items you can make well and quickly beat 80 you can't. Watch Top items in the owner screen after a month and trim the rest.
  • Prices with or without tax. In India, menu prices usually include GST.
  • Prices with or without tax. In Malaysia, choose whether SST and the service charge are added on top or included. SST is charged only after you add an SST number, and the service charge only on dine-in bills.

Questions new cafe owners ask

Do I need GST or SST from the first day?

Usually not. In India, a restaurant generally registers for GST once its turnover passes ₹20 lakh a year (₹10 lakh in some states). In Malaysia, food and beverage businesses register for service tax at RM1.5 million a year. You can still register earlier if it suits you. Ask an accountant about your case.

Can I open before my FSSAI or council licence arrives?

No. In India you need FSSAI registration or a licence before you start selling food, and in Malaysia the council licence comes before opening. Apply early: these are often the longest step.

How much should I spend on the POS?

Start with the Free plan and the tablet or laptop you already have. Free is enough for one counter in a browser. Move to Starter or Pro when you need more devices, offline billing, stock or catering. Set up free. 30 days of Pro from the first real bill or from day 15 if you have not billed yet.

Should I have a self-order kiosk?

If you have a queue at busy times, yes: customers order while your team makes the coffee, and they see photos of everything, which helps them add items. If you're small and quiet, start with the counter and add a kiosk later. It's the same app.

Should I join Swiggy, Zomato, GrabFood or foodpanda?

Delivery apps bring new customers but take a commission on each order. Many cafes set slightly higher app prices to cover it. OrdenZ keeps separate app prices and shows what each app still owes you, so you can see whether it's worth it.

How do I stop cash going missing?

Give each person their own PIN, count a float into the cash drawer at the start of every shift, and count it note by note at closing. OrdenZ shows the difference straight away and sends anything over your limit to the owner to review.

What records should I keep for tax?

Every bill, supplier bills (with photos), and your daily sales. OrdenZ keeps all of these and exports a sales day book, a purchase day book, a GST or SST working sheet (not a filed return) and Tally files for your accountant to import. OrdenZ does not push live into Tally.
